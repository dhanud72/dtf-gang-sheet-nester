Print sizes and placement, with the actual numbers

Two things make a printed garment look professional rather than homemade: the design is the right size, and it sits in the right place. Both are just measurements, and both are easy to get wrong by an inch or two.

Start from the garment, not the artwork

The most common mistake is scaling a design to whatever looks right on screen. A screen has no size. A t-shirt does, and it changes with every size in the run — a design that fills the chest of a small looks stranded on a 2XL.

The practical approach is to fix the size by position first — left chest, full front, back — and then adjust for garment size only where it matters. Most shops print one size across S to XL and a reduced version for youth and toddler.

If a customer gives you no guidance at all, 4 inches for a left chest and 11 inches for a full front will look right on the overwhelming majority of adult shirts. Those two numbers cover most orders.

Where exactly it goes

Left chest

Measure roughly 7 to 9 inches down from the shoulder seam and about 4 inches in from the centre of the collar. A useful shortcut: the top of the design usually lines up with the bottom of the armpit seam.

Note it goes on the wearer's left, which is the right-hand side as you look at the shirt laid out in front of you. Getting this backwards is a surprisingly common and very visible error.

Full front

Centre it horizontally, and place the top edge 3 to 4 inches below the collar seam for adults — about 2.5 inches for youth. Measure from the seam, not from the top of the folded collar, or everything drifts upwards.

Fold the shirt lengthways to find the true centre line. Eyeballing the middle is the other reliable way to produce something that looks slightly wrong without anyone being able to say why.

Full back

Centre horizontally, top edge about 3 inches below the collar seam. Backs can carry slightly more width than fronts because there are no sleeves cutting into the space.

Sleeve

Usually about 3 to 4 inches down from the shoulder seam, centred on the sleeve. Keep the design narrow — a sleeve curves, and anything wide will not press flat across the whole area.

Practical tip

Cut a piece of card to your standard left-chest and full-front positions and use it as a jig. It is faster than measuring every garment and it removes the drift that creeps in when a run gets long.

Adjusting across a size run

Within adult sizes, most shops print one size of transfer for everything. That is reasonable up to about XL. Beyond that a fixed design starts to look small, and on a small it can look crowded.

If you are printing a mixed-size order and want to keep it simple, print one transfer at 11 inches and accept it. If the order is large enough to matter, splitting into two widths — one for S–L and one for XL upward — is the usual compromise.

Things that catch people out

  • Hoodies have a pocket. A full front design has far less usable height before it hits the pouch. Measure the actual gap between collar seam and pocket top before committing to a size.
  • Zips. A centred design on a zip-up hoodie will be cut in half. Either go left chest, or split the artwork deliberately.
  • Collars and seams cannot be pressed. Anything crossing a thick seam, hem or zip will not get even pressure and will lift there first.
  • Ladies' cut shirts are narrower. A full front sized for a unisex adult often needs an inch taken off.
  • Sleeves and legs are curved. Keep those designs narrow and short.

Working out how much film you need

Once the sizes are settled, the film cost is just geometry. A 22 inch roll fits five 4-inch designs across with a small gap. Twenty of them is four rows, so roughly 17 inches of film — call it half a metre with margins.

Rather than working this out by hand, the gang sheet nester will pack the job for you and tell you exactly how much length it takes, and where the waste is. Mixed sizes are where it earns its keep: fitting 2-inch and 11-inch designs together efficiently is not something anyone does well by eye.

Common questions

Which side is the left chest?

The wearer's left, which appears on the right as you look at the shirt laid out facing you. If you are unsure, imagine putting the shirt on — the design sits over the heart.

How far below the collar should a front design start?

About 3 inches on adult sizes, 2 to 2.5 on youth. Measure from the collar seam rather than the top edge of the collar, or the design creeps up and looks like it is climbing towards the neck.

Can I press over a seam or a zip?

Not reliably. Raised areas take all the pressure and the flat areas around them get very little, so the transfer bonds unevenly and lifts at the edges. Keep designs clear of seams, hems and zips.

Should the design size change for every shirt size?

Not usually within adult S to XL — one size works. It is worth a second, larger version for 2XL and above, and a genuinely smaller one for youth and toddler, where a full-size adult print simply will not fit.
